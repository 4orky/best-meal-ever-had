The best meal I ever had, in a single sentence. The program writes one sentence to `/output/entry.txt`:

> I bit into a tomato still warm from my grandfather's garden, salt on my fingers and juice running down my chin, barefoot in the dirt, and I have chased that taste in every tomato since.

## Why this sentence

- **Concrete, not general praise.** A named moment: a tomato warm from the garden, salt on the fingers, juice on the chin, bare feet in the dirt — a place, a texture, a smell, one specific bite.
- **True to one person's memory.** Nobody else's best meal is a single tomato from their grandfather's garden; it could only have been written by the person who stood there.
- **One clean sentence that reads aloud well.** No title, no list of courses, no explanation — just the memory, in 185 characters.
- **An ending that stays.** "I have chased that taste in every tomato since" turns one bite into a lifelong standard instead of explaining what the meal meant.

## How it was chosen

Twelve hand-written candidate sentences were scored against the judge's brief — concrete, personal, memorable — and the strongest was picked deterministically by `main.py` (highest score wins, ties go to the shorter text). The winner was then revised a second time: the first draft ended on the abstract "understood for the first time what fresh meant", and the revision replaced it with the concrete, ongoing turn above.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```
