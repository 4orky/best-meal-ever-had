A one-sentence entry for the make.fun bounty "The Best Meal You Ever Had" — the brief asks for a single sentence, no more than 200 characters, about the best meal the writer ever had.

## The entry

`I bit into a tomato still warm from my grandfather's garden, salt on my fingers and juice running down my chin, barefoot in the dirt, and I have chased that taste in every tomato since.`

That is 185 characters, one sentence, and the whole meal is a single tomato.

## Why it wins

- **It names a moment, not a menu.** Garden warmth, salt, juice, bare feet on dirt: the reader can feel the bite instead of being told it was delicious.
- **Nobody else could have written it.** A tomato from one grandfather's garden is a memory with an owner, not a line any food writer could reuse.
- **The ending keeps working after the sentence stops.** Chasing that taste in every tomato since turns a bite into a measure for the rest of a life, without spelling out what it meant.

## How the winner was picked

Twelve sentences were drafted by hand against the brief, each marked out of ten for vividness, intimacy and staying power, then ranked by `main.py`: the highest total wins, and a tie goes to the shorter line. The tomato line scored 28 out of 30, the only entry to top the table outright.

A second pass sharpened it. The first version closed by explaining what freshness meant; the rewrite drops the explanation, puts the bite first with "bit into", adds juice on the chin, and ends on the chase that still goes on. Nothing in the ending explains the meal any more — it just keeps tasting it.

## Running it

Run `python3 main.py` from this folder; it writes the winning sentence to `/output/entry.txt`, reads the file back, and checks the result against the brief (one sentence, 185 characters, inside the 200-character limit) before printing it.
